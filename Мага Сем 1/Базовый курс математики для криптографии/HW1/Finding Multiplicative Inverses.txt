1) 261^(-1) mod 133
261 = 133 * 1 + 128
133 = 128 * 1 + 5
128 = 5 * 25 + 3
5 = 3 * 1 + 2
3 = 2 * 1 + 1
2 = 1 * 2 + 0
gcd(261, 133) = 1, so the inverse exists.
1 = 3 - 2
1 = 3 - (5 - 3) = 2 * 3 - 5
1 = 2 * (128 - 25 * 5) - 5 = 2 * 128 - 51 * 5
1 = 2 * 128 - 51 * (133 - 128) = 53 * 128 - 51 * 133
1 = 53 * (261 - 133) - 51 * 133 = 53 * 261 - 104 * 133
53 * 261 ≡ 1 (mod 133)
Answer: 261^(-1) ≡ 53 (mod 133)
2) 119^(-1) mod 567
567 = 119 * 4 + 91
119 = 91 * 1 + 28
91 = 28 * 3 + 7
28 = 7 * 4 + 0
gcd(119, 567) = 7 ≠ 1.
Back substitution gives 7 = 4 * 567 - 19 * 119. Since 7 divides both 119 and 567, any 119x - 567y is a multiple of 7 and cannot be 1.
Answer: the inverse does not exist.
3) 17^(-1) mod 81
81 = 17 * 4 + 13
17 = 13 * 1 + 4
13 = 4 * 3 + 1
4 = 1 * 4 + 0
gcd(17, 81) = 1, so the inverse exists.
1 = 13 - 3 * 4
1 = 13 - 3 * (17 - 13) = 4 * 13 - 3 * 17
1 = 4 * (81 - 4 * 17) - 3 * 17 = 4 * 81 - 19 * 17
-19 * 17 ≡ 1 (mod 81), -19 ≡ 62 (mod 81)
Answer: 17^(-1) ≡ 62 (mod 81)
Similarities between 2 and 3:
Example 2 is example 3 multiplied by 7: 119 = 7 * 17, 567 = 7 * 81. The Euclidean algorithm has the same quotients (4, 1, 3, 4), and each remainder in example 2 is 7 times the remainder in example 3. The Bezout coefficients are the same: 4 * 567 - 19 * 119 = 7, divided by 7 gives 4 * 81 - 19 * 17 = 1. Because of the common factor 7, gcd(119, 567) = 7 and no inverse exists, while gcd(17, 81) = 1 and the inverse exists. An inverse of a mod n exists only when gcd(a, n) = 1.
